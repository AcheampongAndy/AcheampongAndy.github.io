Research

Research

Developing statistical and computational methods for complex biological questions.

My research interests center on Bayesian statistical methods, scalable MCMC, stochastic modelling, mathematical biology, computational statistics, and statistical genomics. I am especially interested in the challenges posed by high-dimensional biological data and the need to quantify uncertainty.

Current research

Network-Informed Bayesian Shrinkage Model for Ordinal Biological Outcomes

MPhil research · Supervisor: Dr. Isabel Mensah · Department of Mathematics, KNUST

This MPhil project is developing an ordinal regression model combining Bayesian shrinkage with gene-network information. Planned simulation studies and an application to an existing biological dataset will evaluate predictive performance, variable selection, and uncertainty quantification.

Research experience

Multidrug-Resistant Bacteria in Catfish Farming Systems

Kumasi Veterinary Laboratory

Analyzed fish and water data collected from 16 aquaculture farms using R, including frequencies, percentages, confidence intervals, tables, and figures. A manuscript from this study is in preparation.

2026

Genetic and Statistical Foundations of Coalescent Theory and Its Applications in Population Genetics

Graduate term paper

Examined the Wright–Fisher model and backward-time inference, including Watterson’s estimator, nucleotide diversity, site-frequency spectrum, and Tajima’s D.

2026

Predictive Analytics for Kidney Disease Progression

Undergraduate thesis

Built a logistic regression model to investigate clinical factors associated with chronic kidney disease and assessed its performance using 10-fold cross-validation. The undergraduate thesis reported 97% classification accuracy and a Kappa value of 0.94.
